# Editorial backlog

These are prompts for Christy. Each piece needs her own recollections, wording, and approval.

## Five ideas to develop first

### On Being a Bookish Kid in Detroit

Opening question — What did it feel like to have an afternoon with nowhere to be except a library or Borders?

- Start with one remembered visit. Let a physical detail, an actual book, or the route there set the scene.
- Follow how you chose books before anyone asked you to be discerning. Connect that freedom to the voices you seek out now if the connection rings true.
- Return to a place or book from the opening. Let a specific change carry the ending.
- Which branch or store do you remember most clearly? Who took you there? Did you read in the aisle, take things home, or both?
- What did Detroit make possible for you as a reader? Was there a book you encountered again as an adult that felt different?

### What Giving Museum Tours Taught Me

Opening question — What would you point out first if you were giving a tour of Saarinen House tomorrow?

- Reconstruct the order of one tour, starting with the first room or object that mattered to you.
- Use a remembered visitor question to explore where preparation ends and conversation begins, if a specific exchange comes to mind.
- End with what you would look for on a return visit. Check architectural details against the museum’s records before drafting.
- What surprised you when you first learned to look closely at the house? What did visitors notice that you tended to overlook?
- Did you ever change your route or explanation because of how a group responded? Which detail still stays with you?

### What Data Can’t Tell Us

Opening question — Can you recall a useful metric whose limitations became clear only after somebody asked a different question?

- Choose a public or everyday example where the reader can inspect what was measured.
- Walk through the decisions behind one number, including whose experience was easy to count and what required another kind of evidence.
- Close on the decision that the measurements informed and the question a person still had to answer.
- What were people trying to decide? At what point did the measurement become less helpful? What evidence would have improved it?
- Which details can you confidently share? What is an example where the measurement genuinely helped?

### What an Arts Education Gave an Engineer

Opening question — Is there something you learned to notice at arts high school that you still notice when working with code or data?

- Place one memorable lesson beside one shareable engineering task. Describe the actual work in each scene.
- Examine what transfers between the two practices, such as revising after critique, and what feels different.
- End with a habit you still use, if you can name one in concrete terms.
- What did a teacher ask you to remake or reconsider? What was your reaction at the time?
- When have you used a similar habit with an engineering teammate? What still feels specific to the studio?

### The Art of Being a Beginner Again

Opening question — When was the last time you caught yourself enjoying something before you were any good at it?

- Choose a real instance from soccer, snowboarding, dance, or another activity that actually feels relevant.
- Reconstruct the awkward first steps and identify the moment you wanted to come back.
- Look at what changed on a later attempt. Keep the ending at the scale of that activity and your experience.
- Who was there? What did experienced people take for granted? What mistake did you keep making?
- Did you enjoy starting over, or only enjoy it afterward? What would you willingly be a beginner at next?

## Life & identity

- **In Defense of Detroit.** What do people miss when they talk about Detroit? Begin with an actual remark, take the reader to two places you know well, and end with a part of the city you still want to learn about.
- **Why I Left New York.** What mattered most in the decision at the time? Sketch one ordinary day before the move and one after it. Consider the things you still miss.
- **Making Friends in Your Thirties.** What invitation would you happily get again? Map how a recurring activity or small gesture developed into familiarity, with permission before identifying anyone.

## Technology & thinking

- **The Invisible Work Behind Good Data.** What maintenance task would a reader never see in the finished dataset? Follow one public or illustrative record through its lifecycle and name the decisions that kept it usable.
- **What Four Years in Humanitarian Tech Taught Me.** What changed in how you understood emergency housing? Confirm the tenure first, then use public work and describe one decision where timing or coordination mattered.
- **The Things I Actually Use AI For.** Which tasks would you miss using it for? Keep a short log of genuine uses, then examine the value and friction in three examples.
- **Why I Studied Math but Love Art.** Which mathematical idea and artwork would you put next to each other? Look at what each asks you to pay attention to and where the pairing stops being helpful.
- **Engineering for Humans.** When did a person’s routine change how you approached a system? Trace one shareable decision from what you observed to what you built and what happened after.

## Art & culture

- **How to Enjoy Modern Art Without Understanding It.** What happens if you spend five minutes with one piece before reading the label? Try it, record the observations, and revisit them once you know its context.
- **My Favorite Museums in the World.** What makes a museum worth returning to? Choose only places you have visited, with one remembered detail and a reason to return for each.
- **Buildings I Think About Often.** Which building do you remember from the inside out? Sketch a short itinerary around one material, room, or threshold in each.
- **A Love Letter to Bookstores.** What do you do first when you enter one? Build from a specific shop, a real browsing habit, and a find that surprised you.
- **Books That Made Me Feel Understood.** Which book gave you words for something hard to articulate? Pick a short passage you supply and focus on when and how you met it.

## Field notes

- **Anthropological Observations of San Francisco.** What small public ritual do you keep noticing? Collect a few scenes, vary the neighborhoods or times of day, and leave room for different interpretations.
- **A Highly Unscientific Ranking of Cities.** Which criterion changes the ranking most? Compare places you know through candid categories you actually care about.
- **The Economics of Having Fun.** What do some pleasures cost in money, planning, or energy? Track a few real outings and see where your prior expectations hold up.
- **A Small Collection of Everyday Joys.** Which pleasant thing would have escaped your attention last week? Keep a brief log and turn several entries into small scenes.
- **Things I Changed My Mind About This Year.** Which shift can you trace to a particular moment or conversation? Write down the earlier view and what moved it, including what is unresolved.
- **In Pursuit of a Life Outside of Work.** Which part of an ordinary week would you protect first? Trace a specific activity through the scheduling and effort that give it room.
