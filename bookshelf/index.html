Bookshelf

Books I’ve kept

Books have been a big part of my life. As a kid, I spent many weekends at the library and Borders (RIP) just reading anything that caught my eye. I tend to gravitate towards voices that validate my experience. Some favorites:

  • PachinkoMin Jin Lee PachinkoMin Jin LeeBook details

    A novel that follows four generations of a Korean family as they make a life in Japan.

    What stayed with me was how trauma carries across generations.

    Find this edition at Open Library
  • Everything I Never Told YouCeleste Ng Everything I Never Told YouCeleste NgBook details

    After their teenage daughter is found dead, a Chinese American family in 1970s Ohio begins to reckon with what they never knew about her or one another.

    Seeing an Asian family in the Midwest felt familiar to me.

    Find this edition at Open Library
  • ChemistryWeike Wang ChemistryWeike WangBook details

    A chemistry graduate student starts questioning the future she’s been working toward, both in the lab and outside it.

    I loved how funny the narrator was.

    Find this edition at Open Library
  • The Opposite of LonelinessMarina Keegan The Opposite of LonelinessMarina KeeganBook details

    A posthumous collection of Marina Keegan’s essays and short fiction, including her reflection on the sense of belonging she found at Yale.

    The wanting to belong is what stayed with me.

    Find this edition at Open Library
  • Lab GirlHope Jahren Lab GirlHope JahrenBook details

    A memoir about becoming a scientist, studying plants, and the friendship behind years of work in the lab.

    I learned a lot about how academia works.

    Find this edition at Open Library
